Visiting shooters

In town for a few days, off a ship, or up for the fishing? The range is open to the public, and a 500-yard line with a rainforest behind it is not something you get at home.

Start with the short path: hours → rules → waiver → directions.

Daylight hours, seven days a week. Open to the public. No night shooting — you must be able to see your backstop.

What you need to know

Getting out here

The club is eleven miles north of downtown on North Tongass Highway — about twenty-five minutes by car, and there is no transit that goes past it. A rental car or a taxi both work; arrange the ride back before you leave town, because cell service at the range is unreliable.

Bringing a firearm to Alaska

Airlines require firearms to be checked, unloaded, in a locked hard case, declared at the counter, with ammunition packed to the carrier's rules. Cruise lines generally do not allow firearms aboard at all. Check with your carrier before you plan around it.

If you're arriving empty-handed

On rimfire match days, loaner rifles and ammunition are often on hand. On other days, bring your own firearm or request a visit so someone can meet you.

Ammunition

None is sold at the range and Ketchikan is an island — local supply comes by barge and can be thin. Buy in town the day before rather than counting on it.

Before you come

Everyone shooting here signs the same waiver and follows the same rules, visitor or member. Read Your first visit and the range rules, and sign the waiver ahead of time.

Ask first

Request a visit

Tell the club when you'll be in town and someone will confirm whether a member can meet you out there. This is a request, not a booking.

What happens next

  • Someone replies by email, usually within a few days.
  • They confirm a day, a time and what you need to bring.
  • You sign the waiver before you arrive.
  • Day-use fee is paid at the range. Ask at the shack for the current amount.
