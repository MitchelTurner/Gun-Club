Come-up cards explained (and how to make one)

A come-up card is a cheat sheet for elevation (and sometimes wind) at known distances. It turns “I think hold a little high” into a number you already trusted at the bench.

2026-08-06 · 5 min read · Ketchikan Rod & Gun Club

What belongs on the card

  • Load / velocity note
  • Zero distance
  • Elevation comes for each distance you shoot
  • A simple wind column if you want it
  • Date and weather notes from the day you verified

Build one for KRGC

Use the club’s come-up card tool — it works offline once loaded, which matters when cell service dies before the gate. Start with a verified zero, enter your ballistic data, print the card, then confirm at 100 and 200 when you can.

Verify, do not worship

Ballistic apps and presets are guesses until steel or paper says otherwise. Nominal factory velocities vary by barrel. Chronograph when you can; until then, treat the card as a first draft.

Pair the card with printable sight-in targets and a calm zero session. Then take it to the next club match and pencil corrections in the margin.
