How to zero a .22 LR rifle for the rimfire match

A solid 50-yard zero is the difference between chasing clicks and stacking steel. Here is the process we walk new shooters through on the KRGC rimfire line.

2026-08-01 · 6 min read · Ketchikan Rod & Gun Club

Why fifty yards

At the monthly rimfire match, most stages live around fifty yards. Zero there first. You can build a come-up card for farther steel once the fifty-yard group is honest.

What you need

  • Eye and ear protection
  • A stable rest — sandbags or a bipod
  • Paper target with a clear aiming point (print one from targets)
  • A screwdriver or turret tool that fits your scope
  • One box of the ammunition you will shoot in the match

The process

  1. Set up on the 50-yard line. Bore-sight or start with a rough factory zero if you have one. Keep the muzzle pointed at the berm.
  2. Fire a three-shot group from a solid rest, same point of aim every time. Do not chase each shot.
  3. Measure the group center relative to the bull. Adjust windage and elevation to move the group — not the last hole — onto the point of aim.
  4. Fire another three-shot group. Confirm. One more fine click if needed, then stop.
  5. Change nothing else. Same ammo, same cheek weld, same rest. Write the zero distance on a piece of tape under the scope.

Common mistakes

  • Adjusting after every single shot
  • Zeroing with bargain ammo, then shooting match ammo
  • A wobbly rest that invents “scope problems”
  • Forgetting parallax / focus on the target

Southeast Alaska tip. Rain on the objective lens will throw your group. Wipe glass between strings, and do not breathe on a cold eyepiece right before the shot.

Next steps

Build a simple come-up card for 25, 50, and 100 yards with the KRGC come-up tool, then dry-fire the positions you will use on match day. New to the club? Read Your first visit before you drive out.
