Metallic silhouette for beginners

Silhouette is simple to explain and hard to finish clean: knock the steel down or it does not count. Here is how the KRGC match is shaped for newer shooters.

2026-08-07 · 6 min read · Ketchikan Rod & Gun Club

The animals and the distances

Chicken, pig, turkey, ram — left to right, near to far. At KRGC the silhouette lanes run roughly 40 to 100 metres. Rifle and pistol classes shoot the same ideas with different tools.

What “hit” means

The animal must fall. A loud face-plate ring with a standing target is still a miss. That rule is why solid positions and honest zeros beat speed for new shooters.

How to start

  1. Show up to a silhouette Saturday and tell the RO you are new.
  2. Bring a safe, zeroed rifle or pistol you can hold steady — borrow advice before you buy a specialty gun.
  3. Shoot slow. Call wind. Watch which animals you consistently leave standing.
  4. Log comes in a notebook or on a come-up card.

Practice knockdown timing with the silhouette trainer at home, then prove it on steel. Gallery noise is optional; falling steel is not.

After the match

Help reset. Ask someone who cleaned a bank what they held for wind. Come back — silhouette rewards the people who keep notes.
